René Girard's Mimetic Theory and Healing / Caring

October 9, 2026

I was recently turned onto René Girard and his Mimetic Theory through a YouTube video exploring the philosophies driving our slow (or perhaps accelerating) descent (imprisonment?) into techno-feudalism/fascism/insert-your-preferred-ism.  I had heard Girard's name before but didn't know his story or any of his theories; when I learned he converted to Catholicism through developing his philosophy, I was naturally inclined to learn more about him.

My intention here is not to write on either Girard or Mimetic Theory; I'll just drop two links and an image from one of them for folks to check out at the bottom of this post.  To give mimetic theory a very brief summary, though, for my actual purposes of writing: everything we desire is via imitation of another; this is how we came into "being" as infants, toddlers, teens, etc. - imitating what we saw around us. This does not end in adulthood, though; it is ongoing. Unchecked, it can lead to some serious consequences (see the link below!). 

Why this matters to me (other than being very intellectually engaging and dropping truth nukes): Is this theory applicable to healing as well? Is there a way to adopt it into a care modality? I'm not sure, which is why I turned to this book, Raising Abel by James Alison, published in 1996.  I have a brief respite this week given our "reading week" at Duke Divinity, so I'm thankful for the time to be able to turn to non-academic (though still academic) books for a bit. 

I'm only a few pages in, but so far so good. I'm still trying to figure out the best way of using my time and effort writing here on this site (especially in the form of these blog posts), so I guess this can be a little teaser while also keeping me honest in my reading of it (as well as keeping me writing - a never-ending and always in need of improvement pursuit at divinity school).

Anyhow, I encourage you to check out the links I shared and I'll plan on engaging further with this idea.
